Visakhapatnam Metropolitan Region · Master Plan 2041

The plan protects these lakes. Most of them have no water.

Every water body the statutory master plan draws at 20 hectares or more, pointed at with Sentinel-2 and asked one question: is there still water there? Each was scored on its own wettest observation between October and January, so a cloudy day or a dry week cannot produce a false accusation. Only sites whose imagery actually came back are counted here.

Why this is not a measurement error

Planned sizeDryOf Rate

Every reservoir above 100 hectares still holds water. Below that, close to half do not, and the rate is flat across the three smaller bands. A pointing error would grow steadily as targets got smaller, producing a gradient. Flat, then a cliff, is what a real pattern looks like: nobody fills a reservoir, but a village tank can go quietly.

What is standing on them

Optical imagery cannot answer this. A first attempt classified each bed from spectral indices and called 31 sites built over. It was wrong: at ten metres a bright salt crust and a concrete roof look the same, and it had read Visakhapatnam's salt pans as buildings. That result was deleted, not published.

Radar does not confuse them, because it measures roughness rather than brightness. A building throws a corner reflection straight back at the satellite. A salt pan, a flat bed and open water scatter it away. So the pass was redone with Sentinel-1, terrain corrected, up to four passes per site.

Optical called them buildings. Radar refuses to. That is the exact failure the previous method died of, and this one does not have it. On that basis of the sites here carry hard structure in or against the bed. It is a reason to send someone, not a finding on its own: the sampling window includes the ground immediately around each tank, so a structure at the edge counts.

What this does not say

Sites whose imagery came back blank were dropped, not counted. A third of the first pass returned empty frames, because a scene's bounding box can contain a point that its orbit swath misses. A blank frame cannot tell dry from no-data, so those 32 sites were removed from the claim rather than counted as evidence. Every site shown below has a picture behind it.

This says water is missing. It does not say why, and nor should anyone reading it. An earlier pass tried to classify what now occupies each bed from spectral indices and got it wrong: it read Visakhapatnam's salt pans as buildings. Bare soil, sand, a cropped tank bed and a factory are not separable at ten metres. That classification was removed rather than shown, and the images are here so every call is yours to make.

Positions carry about 200 m of error. Areas are what the plan claims, recovered from a published picture with a 43% median error, so they describe the plan's intent and never a quantity lost.
