What this plan can and cannot answer

The Master Plan 2041 is a statutory instrument carrying ₹1,49,201 crore of capital investment. The 2026 revision covers 4,380 sq km, down from 4,873 in the 2021 draft: a reduction of about 490 sq km, and one of the few draft-to-final changes that can be stated without an error bar, because each figure is published on its own sheet. The plan reaches the public as a picture. This page is that picture, read as far as it can be read.

The sheet dimensions and recovery test below describe the 2021 draft. The selected version is identified above.

What is checkable today

✓
The sheet itself. 43,200 × 16,200 px across 14,462 tiles, recovered in full from VMRDA's own viewer.
✓
The pattern of zoning. Agriculture, forest, the hill ranges and the industrial belt all come back cleanly.
!
Zone at a point, to roughly 17 m, by colour. Good enough to browse, never good enough to cite.
✓
Real coordinates. The sheet carries no graticule, but it is now georeferenced to about 200 m against OpenStreetMap, so any point on it can be located on the ground.
✓
What the plan protects, checked against satellite. Forest, hills, the new Protected Green class and the Kambalakonda eco-sensitive zone: 1,146 sites traced off the sheet, each compared post-monsoon against a 2019 to 2021 baseline, using each site's greenest pass in each year. 190 sites could be assessed and 179 of them still carry their vegetation. No site shows a change that repeats in both of the last two windows. Five are flagged on the 2025 window alone, and of those five, two show clearance you can see in the imagery. Six could not be shown properly and are left unknown.
✓
The corridor to the new airport. A 6 km wide band between the city and Bhogapuram, sampled in 156 one kilometre cells and read exactly like the protected sites. 62 cells are sea or sand. Of the 94 carrying vegetation, five have lost it outright and two more are thinner two years running. The five are the airport itself, where the runway is now visible on imagery; the two are a plotted layout cleared on the coast near Bheemili. The band is a sampling frame drawn by this project, not a road and not a plan feature.
!
Most protected sites cannot be assessed at all. 956 of the 1,146 are narrower inside than the 200 m this sheet is placed to, so a small registration shift changes which ground is inside the outline. They are drawn and they are in the data; they are never counted. Protected Green is the extreme case: 577 sites, 7 assessable, and the assessable ones cover 10 of its 34 traced sq km.
!
Dry water pins. 145 water bodies the plan protects, checked against Sentinel-2 at their wettest pass and against Sentinel-1 for what is on the bed. 73 hold no water: 37 across the region and 36 inside the city. Radar finds hard structure on 3, so the water is going but it is not being built over.

Take the data

Everything this page derived, in the form it derived it. Each file carries the same errors the page does: the sheet is placed on the ground to about 200 m and a recovered area carries 43% median error. Read the ordering, not the totals.

  • Traced zones, Final 2026 22,176 polygons in lon/lat, from 0.5 ha up, with the per-class coverage they were traced at.
  • Traced zones, Draft 2021 56,561 polygons, same form, for comparing the two legends rather than the two geometries.
  • Plan versus ground Every checked protected site across four classes, with its verdict, scene dates, index values, radar figure, image chips and any note from the person who looked.
  • Airport corridor band 156 one kilometre cells between the city and the new airport, each read the same way. A sampling frame, not a road or a plan feature.
  • Protected Green baseline Where the class sits and what it reads today, recorded for comparison in a few years. Not a finding, and not the announced Miyawaki zones.
  • Protected sites Every forest, hill, green, water and eco-sensitive patch over 2 ha, for checking against imagery.
  • Water checks 145 protected water bodies with their Sentinel-2 and Sentinel-1 verdicts.
  • Zoning schedule G.O. Ms. 216 permitted and prohibited lists, per class, as this page reads them.
  • Settlements 4,189 named places with coordinates, from OpenStreetMap.

Reuse them. Attribution to this reader is welcome and not required; attribution of the underlying plan belongs to VMRDA, and the satellite imagery to Copernicus and the European Space Agency.

Why there is no automatic diff

Both versions are here and both are georeferenced, so you can switch between them at any location and the map stays on the same ground. What this will not do is compute how much land changed class, and that is a deliberate refusal rather than a missing feature.

A first attempt said 39% of the region changed. The transition table gave it away: Residential to Mixed use came out at 32.5 sq km and Mixed use to Residential at 32.2. Real decisions do not swap two classes evenly across a region; misalignment does. Counting only cells whose whole 750 m neighbourhood is one class in both sheets left 563 cells of 85,127, and 96% of those were unchanged.

The reason is measurable. 97% of zone patches in both plans are under 400 m across, median about 50 m, while each sheet is georeferenced to roughly 200 m. The error is around eight times the median patch. Comparing parcels at that ratio is not a tuning problem, it is a wall.

So the honest offer is the one a planner actually uses: go to a place, switch versions, and look. At one site, zoomed in, your eye handles the 200 m that arithmetic cannot.

What would fix it

1. Publish the plan as vector layers with a change log, so any two versions can be compared and every edit carries who, when and why.

2. Repair the Final Revised Master Plan download. A statutory document nobody can obtain cannot be complied with.

3. Once there is a baseline, Sentinel imagery gives a free twelve-day check on whether the ground is diverging from the plan.
