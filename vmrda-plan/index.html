What this plan can and cannot answer

The Master Plan 2041 is a statutory instrument covering 4,873 sq km and carrying ₹1,49,201 crore of capital investment. It is published to the public as a picture. This page is that picture, read as far as it can be read.

What is checkable today

✓
The sheet itself. 43,200 × 16,200 px across 14,462 tiles, recovered in full from VMRDA's own viewer.
✓
The pattern of zoning. Agriculture, forest, the hill ranges and the industrial belt all come back cleanly.
!
Zone at a point, to roughly 17 m, by colour. Good enough to browse, never good enough to cite.

What is not

×
Area. Recovered figures miss VMRDA's own Table 17-4 by a median of 43%. Detail below.
×
Any comparison between versions. Two pictures cannot be differenced. When the plan is said to have changed between draft and final, nothing published can settle it either way.
×
The final plan at all. The official download for the Final Revised Master Plan 2041 does not resolve, from inside India or outside it.
×
What has changed on the ground. The base map was interpreted by hand from imagery captured in 2015 and 2017. No refresh cycle is defined, and the capital plan funds none.

The recovery test

Every zone colour was sampled off the published legend, every map pixel classified to its nearest match, and the result measured against VMRDA's published table. 34% of the plan area carries a readable zone colour; the rest is boundary line work and type.

Given the official sheet, the printed legend and the published area table to check against, the zoning still could not be reconstructed to better than half. That is the argument for publishing the vector data, and it is a measurement rather than an opinion.

What would fix it

1. Publish the plan as vector layers with a change log, so any two versions can be compared and every edit carries who, when and why.

2. Repair the Final Revised Master Plan download. A statutory document nobody can obtain cannot be complied with.

3. Once there is a baseline, Sentinel imagery gives a free twelve-day check on whether the ground is diverging from the plan.
